<Section id="he-thong-lam-gi" title="What this is for">

PlanBench answers **one** question: *which navigation configuration
should be deployed in this warehouse* - and answers it with evidence
somebody else can check.

Measuring on a real robot is expensive, slow and unrepeatable: each run
costs tens of minutes, needs a free robot and a clear floor, one
collision damages hardware, and the situation that just broke cannot be
staged again to look at. This platform replaces that with simulation -
**simulation only, no real robot, no safety certification.**

What it adds over running something a few times and picking is three
things: conditions that are locked and checkable, a set of gates kept
separate from the score, and every number carrying a confidence interval
and the number of runs behind it.

</Section>

<Section id="luong-tong-the" title="How the work flows">

Four things, in dependency order:

1. **Declare the world** - map, robot, sensors, missions, thresholds.
   This is the fixed half of a comparison.
2. **Choose what to compare** - two complete navigation configurations.
3. **Run** - both across the same set of episode conditions.
4. **Read** - gates first, score second; then take the result out, or ask
   a second person to review it.

Each step in detail is in [Seven steps](/guide/operation). To see a
result before reading anything else, take
[The first five minutes](/guide/getting-started).

</Section>

<Section id="thuat-ngu" title="The words used here">

Four words used throughout, and none of them is a synonym for another:

- **Deployment** - the declaration of one specific world. Map, robot,
  sensors, missions, thresholds, weights. Everything *held fixed* in a
  comparison.
- **Candidate** - one complete navigation configuration: a global planner
  plus a local controller plus that controller's configuration. Not "an
  algorithm".
- **Episode** - one run: one mission, one seed, one set of moving
  obstacles at defined moments.
- **Comparison** - two candidates run over the **same** episodes,
  producing a decision card.

</Section>
