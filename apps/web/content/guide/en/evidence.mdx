These three decide how far a number on the screen is allowed to go. They
are peers; none needs reading before another.

<GuideTabs slug="evidence">

<div>

Both candidates run over the **same set of episode conditions**: same
seed, same moving obstacles in the same places at the same moments, same
sensor noise. Each set of conditions carries a **checksum**, so "both ran
under the same conditions" is checkable rather than asserted.

The difference is taken **per pair** and only then aggregated - not as
the difference of two detached averages.

Why that matters: the largest source of noise in a navigation benchmark
is usually *which episodes fell to whom*. Pairing removes that before
anything is computed; two detached averages carry it into the result.

Only compare runs with the same checksum. A different checksum is a
different world.

</div>

<div>

Every number carries a 95% confidence interval and **the number of runs
behind it**. The interval is bootstrapped over the paired differences
themselves.

An interval **straddling zero** means this run has not demonstrated the
difference it just reported - even when the mean difference is non-zero
and its sign looks convincing.

<Callout kind="trap">

Reading the sign while ignoring the interval is how a conclusion ends up
sounding stronger than the data allows. "A beats B" is far easier to say
than "A exceeds B by 0.039, 95% interval [0.036, 0.042], over 30 paired
episodes".

</Callout>

The decision card says so outright rather than leaving the reader to
notice.

</div>

<div>

A claim about **mechanism** - *why* A beat B - is not allowed to stand
level with an observation. Four rungs, and only four:

1. **observed** - seen in the data.
2. **associated** - statistically related.
3. **mechanism_verified** - the mechanism checked by a deterministic
   test.
4. **intervention_supported** - something was changed and the result
   moved with it.

**There is no fifth rung for "unknown".** Missing evidence means **no
claim**, not a weak one.

AI's role at this layer is to **propose hypotheses**, because the
combinatorial space is too large for a fixed rule set. It is never the
source of a number, a verification, or a causal conclusion - it proposes,
and the deterministic layer checks and stamps.

</div>

</GuideTabs>
