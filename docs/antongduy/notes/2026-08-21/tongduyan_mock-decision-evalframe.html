Why a blocked candidate still carries a mark, and why the line matters more than the number. There is no invented penalty — any figure we subtracted would be a weighting nobody wrote down, printed as though it had been measured. Instead the field splits in two and never interleaves: collisions are excluded from U_S by contract (HĐ-6) so they cannot be traded against speed, which means a stack that hit something can carry a higher mark than one that did not.

The id stays legible in every state, so the failure case is still usable — the reader selects it by hand. Tested through the pure helper copyDecisionId(id, write), which takes the write function as a parameter, so both branches run in Node with no navigator and no global mock.

A recommendation, when the run produces one
With two candidates through the gates, the verdict card names a winner and prints ΔU with its 95% interval, never without it — a margin whose interval includes zero is consistent with the two being equal, and printing the mean alone turns that into a result.
